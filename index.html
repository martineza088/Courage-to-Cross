Getting oriented

When crossing a bridge brings up fear

For some people, bridges can bring on intense worry or physical anxiety. That experience is real—and it can look different from one person to the next.

01

It has many possible triggers

Height, traffic, feeling enclosed, water, or not being in control can each play a part. Sometimes the reason isn't obvious.

02

Fear is a human response

A racing heart, tense muscles, or a strong urge to avoid a crossing are ways the body can respond to perceived danger.

03

Support can make a difference

If fear is limiting your choices or causing distress, a qualified mental health professional can help you explore options.

A note on the name

“Gephyrophobia” is one word for a fear of bridges.

People may also describe a fear of driving over bridges or of heights. Only a qualified clinician can assess whether a specific phobia or another concern fits your experience.
