You are not the only one

Make room for understanding.

Fear can feel isolating. A little support from a trusted person or a qualified professional may make it easier to talk about what you're experiencing.

Gentle ways to connect

Connection doesn't have to mean explaining everything.

01

Choose someone you trust

You might start with a friend, family member, healthcare provider, or therapist who listens without judgment.

02

Say what would help

Try naming a practical need, like having time to plan a route or being listened to without pressure to cross.

03

Set your boundaries

You can decide what to share, pause a conversation, or ask someone not to push you into a situation that feels unsafe.

A conversation starter

“I want to tell you something that's been hard for me.”

“Bridge crossings can bring up a lot of anxiety for me. I'm not looking for you to fix it right now. It would help if you listened and asked what kind of support I want.”

Online spaces: take care of yourself

Peer groups can offer connection, but they are not a replacement for professional care. Protect your privacy, check group guidelines, and step away from conversations that make you feel pressured or worse.
